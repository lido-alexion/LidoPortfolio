# 1. Purpose

The Live Trading subsystem is responsible for transforming investment decisions generated by StoX into executable broker orders while ensuring security, risk management, auditability, and operational reliability.

This subsystem bridges the gap between analytical decision making and real-world trade execution.

Its responsibility begins after a Recommendation has been generated and ends when the resulting Orders have either been executed, rejected, cancelled, or expired.

---

# 2. Scope

The Live Trading subsystem includes:

- Broker connectivity
- Order generation
- Order basket management
- Order execution
- Execution workflows
- Execution modes
- Trading security
- Trading permissions
- Risk validation
- Trading automation
- Broker communication
- Notifications
- Audit logging
- Operational monitoring

---

# 3. Responsibilities

The subsystem SHALL:

- Convert Recommendations into executable Orders.
- Support multiple execution workflows.
- Execute Orders through supported Brokers.
- Validate all Orders using the Risk Engine.
- Enforce user permissions.
- Record all execution activities.
- Notify users of significant events.
- Support progressive automation.

The subsystem SHALL NOT:

- Generate Recommendations.
- Perform market screening.
- Calculate technical indicators.
- Manage investment strategies.
- Perform portfolio analytics.
- Execute historical backtests.

---

# 4. Architectural Principles

The subsystem shall follow these architectural principles.

## Single Responsibility

Every major component shall own exactly one business responsibility.

## Separation of Concerns

Trading decisions, execution, security, and risk evaluation shall remain independent.

## Deterministic Behaviour

Identical inputs shall always produce identical execution decisions.

## Replaceable Components

Broker implementations shall be replaceable without affecting higher-level components.

## Progressive Automation

Automation shall evolve through increasing levels of user trust rather than immediate full automation.

## Safety First

Safety mechanisms shall always override automation.

---

# 6. Core Components

## Recommendation Consumer

Consumes completed Recommendation Cycles and prepares executable trading intents.

---

## Order Basket Manager

Creates temporary executable Order Baskets.

Allows review, editing, approval, or rejection before execution.

---

## Execution Engine

Transforms approved Order Baskets into broker-specific execution requests.

Coordinates execution but does not communicate directly with broker APIs.

---

## Broker Abstraction Layer

Provides a uniform interface for all supported broker implementations.

No higher-level subsystem shall depend on broker-specific behaviour.

---

## Risk Engine

Evaluates every proposed execution against configured Risk Policies.

Risk evaluation occurs immediately before execution.

The Risk Engine has authority to block execution.

---

## Execution Policy Engine

Determines HOW approved Orders should be executed.

Examples include:

- Market vs Limit
- Retry Policy
- Order Splitting
- Timeout Behaviour
- Partial Fill Behaviour

Execution Policies are independent of Risk Policies.

---

## Security Layer

Responsible for:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Trading Session
- MFA
- Trading PIN
- Permission Validation

---

## Automation Engine

Responsible for scheduled execution without manual intervention.

Automation never bypasses Risk or Security validation.

---

## Monitoring & Audit

Responsible for operational visibility.

Includes:

- Audit Logs
- Broker Health
- Execution Health
- Trading Metrics
- Failure Reporting

---

## Notification Engine

Responsible for informing users about significant trading and operational events.

Notifications never imply approval.

---

# 7. Execution Lifecycle

Every execution follows the same lifecycle.

Recommendation

↓

Order Basket

↓

Validation

↓

Risk Evaluation

↓

Execution Policy

↓

Broker Submission

↓

Broker Response

↓

Portfolio Update

↓

Notifications

↓

Audit Logging

Every execution mode ultimately follows this lifecycle.

Execution Modes only determine where human interaction occurs.

---

# 8. Execution Modes

The subsystem supports four execution modes.

Level 0

Recommendation Only

Level 1

Semi-Automatic

Level 2

Supervised Automatic

Level 3

Fully Automatic

Execution Modes affect workflow only.

They do not modify investment decisions.

---

# 9. Security Model

All Live Trading capabilities shall be protected.

Security controls include:

- User Authentication
- Multi-Factor Authentication
- Trading PIN
- Trading Session
- Trading Permissions
- Administrative Controls

Security-sensitive operations require elevated verification.

---

# 10. Risk Model

Every execution shall pass Risk Validation before Broker Submission.

Risk Policies are independent of:

- Strategy
- Broker
- Execution Mode

Risk validation cannot be bypassed.

---

# 11. Broker Independence

The subsystem shall never assume any specific broker.

Broker-specific implementations shall remain isolated behind the Broker Abstraction Layer.

Future brokers shall be supported without redesigning the execution pipeline.

---

# 12. Operational Principles

The subsystem shall:

- Produce complete audit history.
- Support operational monitoring.
- Recover gracefully from failures.
- Prevent duplicate execution.
- Maintain consistent portfolio state.
- Preserve execution determinism.

---

# 13. Future Compatibility

The architecture shall support future capabilities including:

- Multiple Brokers
- Paper Trading
- Portfolio Rebalancing
- Basket Trading
- Smart Order Routing
- Partial Fill Management
- Derivatives
- International Markets
- Crypto Assets
- Institutional Execution

These capabilities shall require extension rather than redesign.

---

# 14. Out of Scope

The following remain outside this subsystem.

- Strategy Design
- AI Strategy Generation
- Screeners
- Indicators
- Market Analytics
- Portfolio Analytics
- Historical Backtesting
- Tax Calculation
- Financial Planning
